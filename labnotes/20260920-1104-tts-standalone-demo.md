# Native TTS standalone demo

## 2026-09-20 — D5 red boundary and design

- `MIX_ENV=test mix run examples/native_morse_tts.exs E /tmp/vxpipe-native-morse.wav`
  failed with `No such file`, establishing the missing runnable D5 artifact.
- Keep WAV framing and the controlled sink clock inside opt-in scripts; neither is a new
  production speech abstraction.
- The example checks the provider descriptor and uses amplitude 2,048, 6.25% of signed-16
  peak. Its generated artifact path is explicitly outside version control.
- The timing lane is not part of `mix test`. It accepts an explicit concurrency and rounds,
  caps concurrency at half of online schedulers, and separates envelope receipt, generation
  completion and scheduled PCM-duration completion. It makes no acoustic/network/capacity claim.

## 2026-09-20 — Smoke verification

- The `SOS` example wrote `/tmp/vxpipe-morse-sos-d5.wav` with 78,720 PCM bytes and a
  44-byte RIFF/WAVE header. Python's standard `wave` parser confirmed one channel, 16-bit
  samples, 16 kHz, 39,360 frames, 2,460 ms duration and maximum absolute sample 2,048.
- Three bounded timing smokes each ran one worker for one round and passed with 28,800
  generated bytes. First audio was 5,639/10,316/18,162 µs, generation completion was
  55,306/55,457/74,126 µs and controlled sink completion was
  907,101/911,359/923,343 µs. These
  host-pressured samples verify reporting boundaries and are not a latency baseline.
- A concurrency-five invocation on this 8-scheduler machine failed before starting workers
  with `concurrency must be 1..4`, verifying the half-scheduler resource cap.
- Astra review found the first sink deadline used envelope receipt even though validation
  establishes sink authority. First-audio remains anchored at receipt; the corrected sink
  clock starts after successful validation and the third one-worker smoke passes.
- Final Astra review found no remaining blocker. D5 is accepted; D4 and Exit D remain open
  for the separate umbrella gate.
